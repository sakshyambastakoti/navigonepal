Physics Strategy

Passing in physics sounds like a miracle for a huge number of students. Physics can be difficult as it involves many formulas, concepts, derivations, and numerical questions.

How To
Pass Physics

But just to obtain the passing marks, you don't need some magic or miracle. All you need to do is just focus on the right things and study strategically. Here is how you can turn the so-called miracle into reality.

Prithivi Raj Poudel
Research & Development Head • Navigo Nepal

"Passing physics is not a miracle but the result of knowing what to study, understanding what you study, and practicing it properly. You don't need to be a genius on every topic."

01. Understand the Concepts

Do not start by memorizing formulas. Understand what they mean and how they work. For example, instead of only memorizing F = ma, understand what force, mass, and acceleration mean in the real world.

This conceptual grounding makes the formula much easier to remember, derive under examination pressure, and apply to tricky question variations.

02. Prioritize Important Topics

You probably do not have enough time to master every topic across the entire syllabus before your exam. First and foremost, we want to pass the exams—so look at past questions and find the topics that appear often. Focus on those first.

It is better to know the important topics well than to know about all the topics only on the surface level. Master high-yield chapters like Mechanics, Optics, and Electricity first.

03. Break Down Numerical Questions

Many numerical questions are only one or two steps once you break them down. Write down the variables you know, what you need to find, and the formula that connects them.

Given: Initial velocity (u), Final velocity (v), Time (t)
Find: Acceleration (a)
Formula: v = u + at  →  a = (v - u) / t

A long, intimidating question becomes much simpler this way. You also secure partial marking for listing the given parameters and formulas correctly.

04. Learn by Applying

Whenever possible, learn by using the concepts instead of just memorizing them. Solve practice questions immediately after learning a formula, and think about how a concept works in real situations.

You will usually remember a principle significantly better when you actually see how it operates in daily physical phenomena.

05. Practice Meaningfully

Do not just solve as many questions as possible mindlessly. Try different types of questions and solve them without immediately checking the answer.

When you make a mistake, don't simply check the solution and move on. Find out why you made the mistake. Understanding your mistakes is one of the most effective ways to improve.

"Laziness won't help you pass, and blindly memorizing everything won't either. Study smart, stay consistent, and give your efforts a chance to pay off."
— Prithivi Raj Poudel
